Problems of Fake URLs

🛡️Financial Loss

Victims lose millions annually to phishing scams. Attackers steal credit card information, banking credentials, and drain accounts through fake banking websites.

🛡️Identity Theft

Personal information stolen through fake URLs leads to identity theft. Criminals use stolen data to open fraudulent accounts, apply for loans, and ruin credit scores.

🛡️Data Breaches

Corporate phishing attacks lead to massive data breaches. Employee credentials compromised through fake login pages give attackers access to sensitive company systems.

🛡️Malware Installation

Fake URLs often download malicious software onto devices. Ransomware, spyware, and trojans can encrypt files, steal passwords, and monitor user activities.

Steps to Identify Fake URLs

1.Check for HTTPS

Legitimate websites use HTTPS encryption.
Look for the padlock icon in the address bar. However, note that some phishing sites also use HTTPS.

2.Examine the Domain Name

Look carefully at the domain. Phishing URLs often misspell brand names
(e.g., "paypa1.com" instead of "paypal.com") or add extra words (e.g., "secure-amazon-login.com").

3.Watch for Suspicious Patterns

Be wary of URLs with excessive hyphens, random numbers,
unusual domain extension (.tk, .ga), or IP address instead of domain names.

4.Check URL Length

Phishing URLs are often excessively long to hide the real domain or to confuse victims. Be suspicious of unusually lengthy URLs.

5.Hover Before Clicking

Hover your mouse over links to preview the actual URL before clicking.
The displayed text and actual destination may be different.

6.Verify Urgent Requests

Phishing attacks often create urgency ("Your account will be closed!").
Never click suspicious links in urgent emails. Instead, go directly to the official website.
